# Interstellar probe mission concepts: historical and conference reference

**Status:** Working research bibliography, 17 November 2026. Not an editorial endorsement, invitation list, or exhaustive survey.

**Purpose:** Support the Journal of Life Beyond Earth's proposed **Delivery and Transport Systems** scope. Track engineering precedents, primary publications, conference abstracts/proceedings, and named researchers for further literature review and possible professional outreach. The existence of a mission study does not imply technical readiness, biological payload suitability, or support for directed panspermia.

## Scope and distinctions

- **Interstellar precursor / heliosphere missions:** missions into the very local interstellar medium, not necessarily to another star.
- **True interstellar probes:** concepts intended to travel to another stellar system.
- **Biological delivery relevance:** propulsion, transit duration, payload shielding, dust impacts, navigation, deceleration, target selection, containment, and planetary protection. These implications are JLBE research questions, **not claims made by all the cited mission teams**.
- **Conference status:** A conference abstract or presentation is not necessarily a peer-reviewed full paper. Record meeting, year, abstract identifier, authors, and persistent URL separately.

## Mission concepts and people

### Project Daedalus — British Interplanetary Society (1970s; final report 1978)

A detailed study of an uncrewed, fusion-propelled flyby probe to another star. A foundational interstellar mission design, rather than a biological delivery proposal.

- **Primary bibliographic lead:** *Project Daedalus: The Final Report on the BIS Starship Study*, edited by A. R. Martin, *Journal of the British Interplanetary Society* special supplement (1978). Catalog: https://books.google.com/books/about/Project_Daedalus.html?id=36VPmgEACAAJ
- **Institutional record:** U.S. Naval Observatory library catalog: https://1535.sydneyplus.com/genieplus/final/ViewRecord.aspx?record=778a9ae3-c0ce-4b21-ad8c-d9d33ac8b7f6&template=Books
- **Researchers to investigate:** A. R. (Tony) Martin; Alan Bond. Confirm each person's contribution from the original report before describing specific responsibilities.
- **JLBE relevance:** propulsion feasibility, multi-decade transit, flyby vs. capture, high-speed dust environment, payload design.

### Project Icarus — British Interplanetary Society / Icarus Interstellar (launched 2009)

A reassessment of fusion-based interstellar probe design and technological maturity, drawing comparisons with Daedalus.

- **Institutional history:** https://www.bis-space.com/technical-projects/
- **Research publication:** Kelvin F. Long, Richard K. Obousy and Andreas Tziolas, “Project Icarus: The Origins and Aims of the Study,” *JBIS* 64(3), 88–91 (2011); bibliography at https://interstellarresearchcentre.org/papers
- **Related publication:** Long, Obousy and A. Hein, “Project Icarus: Optimisation of Nuclear Fusion Propulsion for Interstellar Missions,” *Acta Astronautica* 68, 1820–1829 (2011); same bibliography.
- **Project-team presentation:** https://www.icarusinterstellar.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/klongicarus.pdf
- **Named researchers:** Kelvin Long, Richard Obousy, Andreas Tziolas, Martyn Fogg, Richard Osborne, Adrian Mann, Andy Presby. The project presentation additionally lists Marc Millis, Greg Matloff, Paul Gilster and Tibor Pacher as consultants.
- **JLBE relevance:** propulsion tradeoffs, destination choice, mission architecture, science payload, risk and repair.

### Interstellar Probe — Johns Hopkins APL-led mission studies (2010s–2020s)

A long-duration precursor mission to the outer heliosphere and very local interstellar medium, **not a probe to another star**.

- **Technical paper:** P. C. Brandt et al., “Humanity’s First Explicit Step in Reaching Another Star: The Interstellar Probe Mission,” *JBIS* 72, 202–212 (2019). https://www.bis-space.com/membership/jbis/2019/JBIS-v72-no06-June-2019%20-%20Subscription%20Copy.pdf
- **AGU Fall Meeting 2020, poster session:** “Interstellar Probe: Pushing the Boundaries of Space Exploration II”; includes “An Overview of the Science Enabled by a Pragmatic Interstellar Probe in the Next Decade” (abstract/program identifier **676698**). https://studio.m-anage.com/agu/fm20/webprogram/Session103634.html
- **EGU General Assembly 2021:** Pontus Brandt et al., “Interstellar Probe: A Mission to the Heliospheric Boundary and Interstellar Medium for the Next Decade,” abstract **EGU21-3308**, DOI https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu21-3308
- **EGU General Assembly 2022:** Brandt and Interstellar Probe Study Team, “Interstellar Probe: A Mission to the Heliospheric Boundary and Interstellar Medium to Understand our Home in the Galaxy,” **EGU22-6802**, DOI https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu22-6802
- **Europlanet Science Congress 2020:** Carey Lisse et al., “What Science Can an Interstellar Probe Mission at Large Heliocentric Distances Achieve With Remote Imaging and In Situ Dust Measurements?”, **EPSC2020-845**, DOI https://doi.org/10.5194/epsc2020-845
- **Researchers appearing on these records:** Pontus C. Brandt, Ralph L. McNutt Jr., Elena Provornikova, Carey Lisse, Kathleen Mandt, Kirby Runyon, Abigail Rymer, Parisa Mostafavi, Drew Turner, Edmond Roelof, Matthew Hill, Michael Paul, Michael Zemcov, Andrew Poppe, Jamey Szalay, Bruce Draine and Mihaly Horanyi. The full lists differ by paper and meeting; consult the linked records for authorship and affiliation.
- **JLBE relevance:** high-reliability long-duration spacecraft, interstellar-medium measurements, radiation/dust environment, power, communications, trajectory and mission operations.

### Breakthrough Starshot — laser-driven lightsail initiative (announced 2016)

Concept for sending very small, rapidly accelerated probes toward the Alpha Centauri system; distinct from heliosphere precursor missions.

- **Official launch record:** https://breakthroughinitiatives.org/events/2
- **Project leadership and advisers:** https://breakthroughinitiatives.org/news/12
- **Named researchers and project figures for literature follow-up:** Pete Worden, Avi Loeb, Philip Lubin, Harry Atwater, Kevin Parkin, Geoffrey Landis, Greg Matloff, Lou Friedman, Jim Benford, Mason Peck, and others listed by the initiative. **Do not equate advisory membership with authorship of a specific design paper.**
- **JLBE relevance:** acceleration loads, tiny payloads, thermal management, high-speed interstellar dust impacts, flyby observation and possible deceleration constraints.
